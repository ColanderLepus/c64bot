# M4: Planner for levels without guards (solver idea 2)

**Status:** Started 2026-11-16. Steps agreed 2026-11-24; P1, P1b and P2 done; P3 under way again, with a step 4.

The first milestone where the bot plays on its own. It reads the level from RAM, plans a route to every piece of gold and then to the exit, and follows the route with the joystick, in real time. Guards come in M5. No level on the disk is without guards, so the bot starts levels with their guards taken out (P1).

## Tasks

- [ ] Model the level as a graph using the movement rules (walk, climb, rope, fall, dig and drop)
- [ ] BFS/A* to each piece of gold, then to the exit ladder
- [ ] Debug view showing the grid and the planned path
- [ ] Debug mode (moved from M1 T9): the real-time loop with a different rule, one frame per key press in the window, and a key to switch between that and real time. Check: each press advances the game by one frame, and holding the key runs it in slow motion.
- [ ] Test on real levels with unlimited lives on, starting each at the level under test with `GameStarter.StartLevel` (D13; no invincibility and no C=, D6)
- [x] Levels without guards: start any level with its guards taken out
- [ ] `IPlayer` and the loop that runs the bot (its shape was left to M3 or M4, and M3 was dropped)

## Open points

To settle before P4 (Claude's suggestion first). The one about writing the level's data is settled: D13 and D16.

1. **`IPlayer`'s shape** (P4). Suggestion: the loop calls the bot on the emulation thread after every frame, with the game state (none in a frame that can't be read, D14), and the bot answers at once with the joystick for the next frame, from the route it's following. Finding a route takes milliseconds to seconds (P3 step 2), too long for between two frames, so the bot plans on a thread of its own: the next route, from where the current one ends, while he walks it (D19). If it isn't ready when he gets there, he waits; without guards that's safe.
2. **R2.** Suggestion: split up `Program.cs` in P4, when the app gets the bot.

## Notes

- **Research for P1** (headless, 2026-11-16): no level on the disk is without guards; the game builds a level's guards from the guard tiles in its data; and a level written into the game's level buffer is set up without the disk, the same as from the disk, 150 frames sooner, and repeatably. The details are in [lode-runner.md](../lode-runner.md): "Guards per level", "Levels on the disk", "How a level is set up" and "A level put into the buffer".
- **Research for P2** (headless, 2026-11-25): the rules of one pass, taken from the disassembly, were raced against the running game on all 150 levels without guards, from random starting places and with random joystick input, diagonals and digging included: 1,127,032 passes in 2,400 runs, without a difference. Each of 24 rules was then broken on purpose, and the race noticed 22 (one of them because the model crashed). The other two make no difference here: "falls while digging" (what lets him start a dig also holds him up while he digs) and "up before down" (a joystick can't be pushed both ways). Not covered: guards, the keyboard, changes of the game's speed.
- **The race again, strict** (headless, 2026-11-25, after P2 step 2): every state `GameState.TryRead` gives must be exactly what the rules give for that pass. All 150 levels without guards, 1,200 runs, 563,787 passes, 833,826 states: without a difference, and never more than one state the rules allowed. Without skipping half-done frames, every run parts.
- **Moves on the real levels** (headless, 2026-11-26, P3 step 1): from the first 3,000 states reached on each of the 150 levels without guards, digs included, there were 1.6 million moves of 58 million passes, worked out in 20 seconds. Most of those passes were waits: with a hole open, waiting until it closes is always a move, and it can take up to 180 passes. Something to keep in mind in step 2, where the search is measured.
- **Routes on the real levels** (headless, 2026-11-26, P3 step 2): taking the nearest gold each time, without looking out for traps (that's step 3), the planner completes 68 of the 150 levels without guards. On 64 it gets stuck with gold left, and on 18 a search gives up. A route is mostly found in milliseconds: half the levels never needed more than 6 ms for one, nine in ten less than 4 seconds, and the slowest took 14 seconds (375,000 places). Gold four bricks down in a plain brick floor takes about a second. What made the search slow was holes: on a brick floor he can dig anywhere, and every mix of holes is a new place. The estimate of the least time still to go cut that down: the four-deep pit went from 50 seconds to 0.6, and the first gold on level 103, behind a long walk round to a ladder, from giving up to 23 ms. Level 148 can't be done without its guards: its gold is up on platforms with nothing to climb, and the only ladders are the hidden exit ladders. Checking first whether he can get there at all ended the searches that started where he's stuck: before it, 31 gave up, after it 18. Those 18 start where the check can't rule the gold out, since it counts every brick he could dig as dug at once, and only the search finds out that the holes don't stay open long enough. Runs from the graph in the search: the four-deep pit took 937–1,123 ms instead of 649, level 125 190–357 ms instead of 121.
